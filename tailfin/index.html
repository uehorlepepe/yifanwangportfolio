A Hybrid Experimental-Computational Framework for Tailfin Geometry Optimization in Low Reynolds Number Flight

A closed-loop study of tailfin (vertical stabilizer) planform optimization for low Reynolds number flight (Re ≈ 50k–100k). The pipeline connects parametric CAD, transition-modelled OpenFOAM CFD, a Gaussian-process surrogate with a Pareto search, and wind tunnel validation. The first three stages are complete; the headline result is an optimized fin that buys 40% more directional stability for 14% more drag versus an RQ-11 Raven style baseline.

Overview

What the project is doing

At the Reynolds numbers where small UAVs and hand-launched aircraft operate, conventional tailfin rules of thumb stop being reliable. Laminar separation bubbles, low aspect ratios, and lightly loaded surfaces change the trade between directional stability and drag. This project asks a concrete question: for a fixed low-Re flight envelope, what planform geometry gives the best stability for the least drag penalty?

To answer it, the work closes the loop between computation and experiment. A parametric CAD generator builds candidate fins, a frozen OpenFOAM template with transition modelling (kOmegaSSTLM) evaluates them, and a machine-learning surrogate makes the design space cheap to search. The result is a small set of Pareto-optimal geometries that are then handed to the wind tunnel for validation rather than guessed at.

Design of experiments uses a Latin hypercube over fin sweep (15–35°), aspect ratio (1.5–3.0), and taper ratio (0.4–0.8). All 30 cases converged, and the surrogate was confirmed to 2.9% error against held-out CFD, giving enough confidence to move from screening to a physical test campaign.

Findings

What the results show so far

  • Small, lightly swept, low-taper fins win on drag, because wetted area dominates at this scale.
  • The predicted optimum (sweep 16.0°, AR 1.54, taper 0.42) delivers roughly +43% side force per degree and +40% stiffer yaw than the Raven NACA 0009 baseline, at a cost of about +14% drag.
  • Three full campaigns (axial, sideslip to ±20°, and 60° delta fins) all stayed attached with no mean-flow reversal, which scopes exactly what the tunnel needs to test.
  • The laminar separation bubble proxy resisted every surrogate tried; the Sobol split explains why by separating the area-driven drag response from the taper-driven bubble response.
Roadmap

Next steps

  1. Print the baseline and optimized fins and run the tunnel campaign — force balance over sideslip 0–15° with oil-flow for the LSB footprint.
  2. Run a sideslip-times-geometry campaign to extract per-planform stability derivatives.
  3. Write up the full framework and results as the manuscript.
